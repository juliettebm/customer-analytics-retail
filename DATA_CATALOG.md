# Data catalogue: product catalogue and transactions (Online Retail II)

To read before starting a cleaning effort or a catalogue monitoring dashboard. Centred on the **product** (`StockCode`, `Description`) rather than on the customer. The customer-side reference is in notebooks 01 to 05.

## 1. Business domains

| Domain | Fields | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Product catalogue (master data) | `StockCode`, `Description` | Product reference, the core of this catalogue |
| Transaction | `Invoice`, `Quantity`, `Price`, `InvoiceDate` | Transactional data, volatile |
| Customer | `Customer ID` | Customer reference, detailed in notebooks 01 to 05 |
| Geography | `Country` | Country reference |

## 2. Field dictionary

| Field | Type | Business definition | Quality finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Invoice` | str, 6 digits, prefixed `C` when cancelled | Invoice or order identifier | 19,494 lines (1.8%) are cancellations (prefix `C`) |
| `StockCode` | str | Product identifier, the catalogue key | 5,305 unique codes, of which 62 do not follow the standard 5-digit format (see Q1) |
| `Description` | str | Product label | 4,382 lines (0.4%) have no description: 353 codes have no valid description anywhere (never described, see Q3) and 2,092 codes are missing it on some rows only, recoverable from their other rows (see Q4); separately, 1,230 product codes carry several different labels across lines (see Q2) |
| `Quantity` | int | Quantity sold, negative when cancelled | 3,455 lines have a negative quantity without being cancellations (see Q7) |
| `InvoiceDate` | datetime | Transaction date and time | December 2009 to December 2011 |
| `Price` | float (GBP) | Unit price | 6,207 lines priced at or below 0 (see Q5) |
| `Customer ID` | float (identifier) | Customer identifier | 243,007 lines (22.8%) have no customer identifier: guest purchases, out of scope for customer-level KPIs (see Q6) |
| `Country` | str | Delivery country | Nothing to report |

## 3. Business glossary

**Non-product code.** A `StockCode` that does not designate a physical article sold, but an accounting adjustment, a fee, a test, or a commercial gesture such as postage or a discount. It pollutes the product catalogue unless it is explicitly separated.

**Catalogue anomaly.** A product whose entry is missing or inconsistent, meaning no description or several labels for the same code, independently of how well it sells.

**Business impact of an anomaly.** In this project, measured as the revenue generated by the product codes affected by a catalogue anomaly, not as a qualitative estimate. That figure is what prioritises the backlog (`BACKLOG.md`).

## 4. Quality rules identified

Q1 to Q4 are audited together in notebook 06, section 3 ("Catalogue quality audit"), and numbered there in that order — this table keeps the same numbers rather than reassigning them, so a reader can move between the notebook and this file without translating. Q5 and Q6 come from notebook 01's own price and customer-identifier findings; Q7 is notebook 06, section 4.

| Rule | Finding | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|
| **Q1: non-product codes mixed into the catalogue** | 62 of the 5,305 `StockCode` values do not follow the standard 5-digit format. After checking each label individually, only 17 are genuinely fees, adjustments or tests (`POST`, `BANK CHARGES`, `ADJUST`, `AMAZONFEE`, `DOT`, `M`, `C2`, `TEST001`, `TEST002` and others). The remaining 45 (`PADS`, `SP1002`, `DCGS*`, `gift_0001_*`) are real products despite their non-standard code | Only the 17 confirmed codes are separated from the product catalogue. The other 45 are kept: filtering on format alone would have wrongly excluded real articles |
| **Q2: label inconsistency** | 1,230 product codes, 23% of the catalogue, carry several different descriptions across lines: typos, variants, updates that were not applied retroactively | Requires a deduplication rule, for instance the most frequent description per code. Documented here so the choice is traceable rather than hidden in the code |
| **Q3: never described (orphan)** | 353 product codes have no valid description on any of their rows. Kept separate from Q4 rather than folded into a single "missing description" figure, because the revenue check gives a different verdict for each: these 353 codes carry **£0 of measurable revenue** — noise (test rows, residual adjustments), not a commercial loss | Low priority for correction: no revenue is attributable to these codes, so fixing them does not change any business number |
| **Q4: partially missing (recoverable)** | 2,092 product codes are missing their description on some rows only; the label can be recovered by joining on another row of the same code that does carry it | A join-and-fill rule resolves this automatically; no manual labelling needed |
| **Q5: inconsistent prices** | 6,207 lines priced at or below 0 | Not to be deleted blindly: some are legitimate cancellations, others are data entry errors. The two are separated in the notebook |
| **Q6: missing customer identifier** | 22.8% of lines have no `Customer ID`, corresponding to guest purchases | Out of scope for any customer-level analysis, but valid for the product-level analysis carried out here |
| **Q7: stock adjustments disguised as transactions** | 3,455 lines have a negative `Quantity` without being cancellations, since `Invoice` carries no `C` prefix. All are priced at zero and labelled *lost*, *damages*, *short* or *mixed*. Found in the univariate analysis rather than in the initial Q1 to Q4 audit: these codes follow the standard format, so Q1 does not catch them | Does not affect revenue calculations, since the price is zero. To be excluded explicitly if `Quantity` is reused on its own, for instance to measure physical volume moved, without filtering on price |
